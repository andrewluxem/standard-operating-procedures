# Procedure design guide

## SOP anatomy

A usable procedure answers:

- Why does this process exist?
- What is inside and outside its scope?
- What observable event starts it?
- What observable state ends it?
- Which roles act, decide, approve, check, and receive the result?
- Which access, training, information, and materials are required?
- Which ordered actions produce the outcome?
- What happens at each decision, exception, and failure?
- Which controls show that the process met its standard?
- Which records are created, where are they kept, and how long are they retained?
- Who owns the version, approval, effective date, and next review?

## Executable-step test

Each step should contain:

1. One observable action.
2. The responsible role.
3. The required input.
4. The output or evidence produced.
5. A due date, response target, or explicit statement that no target was supplied.
6. The next step or failure response.

Avoid combine, handle, manage, process, and review when they hide several actions. Split the work until a trained reader can tell whether the step happened.

## Decision points

Write a decision as a question with evidence-based conditions. Name the yes path, no path, and authorized decider. If judgment is allowed, state its boundary and required record.

## Exceptions and escalation

An exception needs:

- the condition that triggers it;
- the immediate safe response;
- what work must stop;
- who receives the escalation;
- the response date or service target;
- the record that preserves the decision.

Do not infer an emergency response from a normal process.

## Controls and evidence

Controls may be approvals, reconciliations, peer checks, samples, tests, logs, or sign-offs. Name the standard, checker, evidence, and frequency. A checklist box without evidence may track activity but cannot prove quality.

## Change governance

Every SOP names an owner, version, effective date, approver, and next review date. Record what changed and why. Retire superseded versions according to the user's policy; do not invent a retention rule.
